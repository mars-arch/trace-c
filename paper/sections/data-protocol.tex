\section{Data and evaluation protocol}

The study uses five half-hourly NESO demand-side streams and a sixth stream
formed by aggregating one-second system-frequency data to the maximum absolute
deviation from 50 Hz in each half-hour
\citep{neso2019historicdemand,neso2019systemfrequency}. Data are used under
the NESO Open Data Licence \citep{nesoOpenLicence}, with the exact attribution
``Supported by National Energy SO Open Data.'' Source checksums,
transformations, and report-generator provenance are committed alongside the
machine-readable evidence.

January--April 2019 is the fit segment. The remainder of 2019 is a development
year in which window width, trailing-history parameters, and channel choices
were disclosed as test-informed. The 2020 run was frozen before inspecting its
events and is the blind hold-out. Windows are non-overlapping and scored
causally against strictly prior windows. Event ranks are evaluation labels, not
features; the outage, storm, and lockdown annotations are documented
independently
\citep{ofgem2020poweroutage,metoffice2020stormseason,metoffice2020stormalex,ukgov2020lockdown,ngeso2021endyear}.
A storm name attached to a high-ranked window is a post-ranking interpretation.

External baselines use the same six streams, windows, fit dates, causal score
direction, and event intervals. They were assembled after the frozen TRACE-C
2020 results were visible and therefore are a post-hoc comparison, not a
second blind experiment. Event opportunity counts are reported because the
blackout, storms, and lockdown transition cover unequal numbers of windows.
