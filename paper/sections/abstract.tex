Operational telemetry may be ordinary stream by stream yet unusual in its
cross-stream configuration. TRACE-C addresses this setting with strictly-prior
online scoring. Same-regime rolling median/MAD residuals feed three window
channels: a maximum normalized local sum, a Gaussian dependence contrast, and
a worst standardized AR(1) innovation. Upper-tail channel ranks are aggregated
by a Fisher construction, and the aggregate is ranked against earlier
aggregates. The resulting conformal/rank $p$-values are selection quantities,
not estimated event probabilities or posterior confidence. The historically
named
``copula'' channel is evaluated directly on robust-z residuals; because the
shipped method applies no probability-integral or normal-score transform, it is
a Gaussian copula-form log-density contrast rather than a literal copula
density.

We evaluate six Great Britain grid streams: five half-hourly demand-side series
and a half-hourly maximum-deviation summary of system frequency. January--April
2019 is designated for fitting, July--December 2019 is development evidence,
and the unchanged detector is run through a frozen 2020 hold-out. TRACE-C ranks
Storm Atiyah first in development but ranks the short 9 August frequency event
143rd; reconstruction baselines rank that event first. In 2020, its best
lockdown-transition window is rank 45 on 28 March, while no window is selected.
Empirical rank counts are descriptive diagnostics rather than coverage or
false-discovery proofs. The mixed findings expose the consequences of temporal
dependence, record-rule saturation, sensor choice, and aggregation resolution.
