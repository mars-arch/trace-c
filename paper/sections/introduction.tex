\section{Introduction}

Grid operators monitor many streams whose marginal behavior changes with
season, operating regime, and demand. A useful alert should identify a
coherent departure across streams while preserving a causal scoring rule and a
calibrated indication of rarity. This is difficult when the data are
autocorrelated and when the event of interest is a short transient hidden by a
coarse aggregation interval.

TRACE-C (temporal relational anomaly detection with copula calibration) makes
three design commitments. First, it preserves magnitude in robust marginal
residuals rather than replacing a deep excursion with its rank. Second, it
calibrates every channel against a trailing set of strictly prior windows,
which exposes rather than silently assumes exchangeability. Third, it separates
the development year from a frozen 2020 hold-out. The resulting detector is an
operational ranking and triage aid, not a claim that every small conformal
$p$-value has nominal time-series coverage.

Our contribution is an auditable protocol and evidence package: a
copula-informed relational score, causal rank calibration, explicit selection
fallbacks, and a reproducible comparison against common anomaly detectors.
The results are intentionally mixed. TRACE-C detects relational weather-like
episodes well, whereas direct reconstruction can win on an abrupt frequency
transient. That distinction is informative about the sensor set and resolution
as much as about the algorithm.
